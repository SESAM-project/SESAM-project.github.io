abstract: >-
  Context: The EU Cyber Resilience Act (EU CRA) introduces cybersecurity obligations for products
  with digital elements, including secure development, vulnerability handling, software
  supply-chain transparency, and technical documentation. Objectives: To investigate how companies
  are preparing for the EU CRA, with particular attention to software supply-chain security, product
  security, vulnerability management, and readiness to demonstrate conformity. Methods: We
  conducted a qualitative interview study with nine practitioners from two large enterprises (LEs)
  and two small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) located in Sweden. The interviews covered
  current security practices, EU CRA interpretation, compliance planning, evidence artefacts, and
  organisational challenges. We analysed the material using thematic analysis. Results: The four
  cases show uneven EU CRA readiness. The two LE cases reused existing standards to different
  degrees, but one remained fragmented while integrating new product-cybersecurity routines. The
  two SME cases ranged from reactive developer-led security to documented workflows embedded in
  engineering. Across the cases, relevant artefacts existed but were not always maintained as
  reusable compliance evidence. Conclusion: While strategies to prepare for the EU CRA differ
  across companies, we propose recommendations for practitioners, tool providers, and regulators
  seeking to support future regulatory compliance.
